Wiring at a glance

The L293D motor shield sits on the Uno and owns pins 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11 and 12. Everything else plugs in here. The robot finds the optional parts by itself at start-up.

PinWhatNotes
A0 / A1HC-06 BluetoothAs the kit ships. The app and the sticks talk to it.
A2 / A3HC-SR04 sonar (echo / trig)Used only while no sensor is wired straight to A2 / A3.
A4 / A5I2C (SDA / SCL)IMU, laser and ADS1115 boards, see below.
D2IR remote receiver (S)Middle pin to 5V, "-" to GND. D2 has no header on the shield: solder to its pin.
D9 / D10Servo headersFree digital pins (no PWM: Timer1 times the IR remote).
D13Heartbeat LEDFree if you set USE_HEARTBEAT 0.

On the I2C bus (A4 / A5)

Give every board 5V into its VIN. The 3.3V boards (IMU, laser) go through a BSS138 level shifter: HV 5V, LV 3.3V, SDA and SCL on two of its channels.

AddressBoardNotes
0x68MPU-6050 (GY-521)Heading hold. Keep the robot still for a second at power-on.
0x28or BNO055ADD to GND (the cheap boards default to 0x29). PS0 and PS1 must read 0 V for I2C mode.
0x29VL53L0X laserDistance ahead, wall stop at 120 mm. Peel the film off the window.
0x48-0x4AADS1115 x3ADDR to GND / VDD / SDA. 8 line sensor channels and the light sensors. Powered from 5V.

Fitting it all in

The Uno has 32 KB for the sketch, and the default build uses about 32.2 KB. Calibration runs live in a separate program, calibration.ino, which saves its numbers in the robot's EEPROM for this one to read. Every optional part is a USE_... line at the top of the sketch: 1 = in, 0 = out. Lesson 10 turns this into a challenge.
