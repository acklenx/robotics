Lesson 08 · 60 minutes

Following the Line

A feedback loop you can see

Today's goal

Explain, while watching the robot, why it wiggles down the line instead of driving straight along it.

Ideas in this lesson

  • Feedback: measure, correct, measure again
  • Correcting always means overshooting a little
  • Two sensors give you four possible situations
  • The wiggle is not a bug — it is the loop working

Sense → Think → Do

A tight SENSE-THINK-DO loop running many times a second, right in front of you.

How the session runs

  1. Lay a black tape line with a gentle curve. Press 4 on the remote or Line tracking in the app.
  2. Watch the two dots on the phone while the robot drives. Say out loud what it sees and what it does.
  3. Fill in the table: left black → turn left; right black → turn right; neither → straight; both → stop.
  4. Make the curve sharper until the robot loses the line. Find the tightest curve it can hold.
  5. Change the surface or the lighting. Notice the sensors are reading reflection, not colour.

Try this

  • Design a track the robot definitely cannot follow, then explain exactly why in sensor terms.
  • Add a gap in the line. What does the robot do? What SHOULD it do?
  • Could you write the line follower yourself with if-blocks? Try it, then compare with the built-in one.

When it goes wrong

What you seeWhat it means
The robot runs off a black line on a dark floorThe sensors compare reflection. Dark floor, dark line, no contrast, no chance.
It wiggles violentlyThe correction is strong relative to the speed. Lower the speed and watch the wiggle shrink.
Notes for the grown-up

The wiggle IS the lesson. Every control system overshoots; a thermostat does it in temperature and you cannot see it. Here it is right there on the floor.
