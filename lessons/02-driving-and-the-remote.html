Lesson 02 · 60 minutes

Driving & the Remote

Commands, control, and who is in charge

Today's goal

Drive the robot three different ways and explain what a command actually is.

Ideas in this lesson

  • A command is a message, not a wish — something has to receive it
  • Two controllers, one robot: first one to act owns it
  • STOP always wins, from anybody
  • Dead-man control: hold to go, release to stop

Sense → Think → Do

Still mostly DO, but now you can see the SENSE half on the phone while you drive.

How the session runs

  1. IR remote first: arrows drive, OK stops, numbers pick modes. No phone needed — this is the controller that works when the wifi does not.
  2. Connect a phone to the Workshop app. Watch the console: every button you press writes a line.
  3. Drive with the joystick. Notice the motor bars move as you steer.
  4. Two kids, one robot: one on the remote, one on the phone. Whoever acts first owns it; the other gets BUSY. Try to break it.
  5. Press 0 on the remote to release control, then let the other person take over.

Try this

  • Drive a figure-8 around two chairs without touching either.
  • Have a partner shout directions while you drive with your eyes closed. Notice how bad the delay makes you.
  • Find the slowest speed the robot will still move at. Why does it stall below that?

When it goes wrong

What you seeWhat it means
The phone will not connectBluetooth AND location must be on for Android Chrome. The page must be https or localhost.
Robot drives, then stops on its own after a momentThat is the dead-man safety: manual driving expires if the robot stops hearing from you. Hold the button, do not tap it.
Notes for the grown-up

The arbitration demo is worth the time. Kids fighting over one robot is a management problem right up until it becomes the lesson.
