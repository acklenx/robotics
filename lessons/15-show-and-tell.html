Lesson 15 · 60 minutes

Show & Tell

Explaining your robot to somebody who has never seen it

Today's goal

Explain what your robot does, how it knows, and what still beats it — to an audience that was not in the club.

Ideas in this lesson

  • If you can explain it, you understand it
  • The interesting part of engineering is the trade-off, not the success
  • Demos fail; a good presenter has a plan for that
  • Share: this is the third word of Make. Learn. Share.

Sense → Think → Do

Everything, narrated by the people who built it.

How the session runs

  1. Plan the demo: one behaviour, ninety seconds, one clear thing to point at.
  2. Prepare the sentence: 'It senses ___, decides ___, does ___.'
  3. Prepare the honest bit: 'It fails when ___, because ___.'
  4. Rehearse a failure: what will you say if the robot does nothing on the day?
  5. Present to families, another class, or each other. Take questions.

Try this

  • Explain your robot to somebody younger than you without using the word 'code'.
  • Answer honestly: which parts did the robot decide, and which did you decide in advance?
  • What would you build next if the club ran another ten weeks?

When it goes wrong

What you seeWhat it means
The robot misbehaves in front of an audienceFresh batteries, calibration re-run on the actual demo surface, and a rehearsed sentence about why demos are hard.
A group cannot say what their robot doesSend them back to the one-sentence rule from lesson 14. It is never too late to write it.
Notes for the grown-up

Invite people. The difference between a club activity and a piece of real work is an audience, and the questions from a parent who has never seen the robot are exactly the questions that reveal understanding.
