The sketch behind the Workshop app, Sticks and the sensor boards. The class runs ir.ino for now; this one comes back later. Download mecanum_holonomic.ino (0.10.80).

wiring at a glance

This is the pin plan for mecanum_holonomic.ino and its sensor boards. ir.ino uses a simpler one (IR receiver on D10, light sensors on A3 / A4 / A5): see Wiring.

The L293D motor shield sits on the Uno and owns pins 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11 and 12. Everything else plugs in here. The robot finds the optional parts by itself at start-up.

the I2C bus (A4 / A5)

Give every board 5V into its VIN. The 3.3V boards (IMU, laser) go through a BSS138 level shifter: HV 5V, LV 3.3V, SDA and SCL on two of its channels.

AddressBoardNotes
0x68MPU-6050 (GY-521)Heading hold. Keep the robot still for a second at power-on.
0x28or BNO055ADD to GND (the cheap boards default to 0x29). PS0 and PS1 must read 0 V for I2C mode.

0x48-0x4AADS1115 x3ADDR to GND / VDD / SDA. 8 line sensor channels and the light sensors. Powered from 5V.

fitting it all in

The Uno has 32,256 bytes for the sketch, and the default build uses 32,126 of them. Calibration runs live in a separate program, calibration.ino, which saves its numbers in the robot's EEPROM for this one to read. Every optional part is a USE_... line at the top of the sketch: 1 = in, 0 = out. Lesson 10 turns this into a challenge.

Light sensors on mecanum_holonomic

mecanum_holonomic.ino can do it too, set up from the Mecanum Workshop app. On the Sensors tab, pick the preset 2 front corners on A4 A5, then tap Save to robot. Run Calibrate (bc), then Seek (bf), or press # on the remote. Its IR receiver must be on D2, and A4/A5 must be free (no gyro). To make it run from the light, set "In brighter light the reading goes" to DOWN (kbh=0;). Set it back to UP to follow again.

For the firmware details, see the CONFIG section at the top of mecanum_holonomic.ino. On mecanum_holonomic.ino you can also use 4 LDRs (A2-A5) so the robot slides toward the light from any side. That needs the sonar and the gyro unplugged.
