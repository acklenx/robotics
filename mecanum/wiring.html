The whole robot

The Uno sits at the bottom and the L293D motor shield plugs on top of it. Everything else plugs into the shield. The shield uses pins 3 4 5 6 7 8 11 12 for the motors. The free pins are 2, 13, A0-A5, plus the two servo headers (pins 9 and 10).

Two light sensors (LDRs): follow the light, or run from it

Before you start:
  • Light seeking runs on mecanum_holonomic.ino, not ir.ino. Flash mecanum_holonomic.ino. The motion calibration is kept, because it lives in the robot's memory.
  • The LDRs use A2 and A3, which is where the sonar plugs in. Unplug the sonar's Echo and Trig wires. If they stay plugged in, the sonar fights the LDR readings.
  • On mecanum_holonomic.ino the IR remote must be on D2. If your robot's receiver is on D10 for ir.ino, move its OUT wire to D2.

The circuit (one per LDR)

An LDR is a resistor that changes with light: high resistance in the dark, low in bright light. On its own the Uno cannot read a resistance. So each LDR gets a partner resistor, making a voltage divider, and the Uno reads the voltage in the middle. LDRs and resistors have no polarity, so either leg can go either way.

  1. One leg of each LDR to 5V.
  2. The other leg of the left LDR to A2. The other leg of the right LDR to A3.
  3. A resistor (10k to start) from A2 to GND, and another from A3 to GND.

Wired this way, brighter light gives a higher reading (0 to 1023). That is the app's default setting.

Easiest build: on each signal wire, twist or solder the LDR leg and the resistor leg together at the pin. The resistor's other leg goes to a GND pin, and the LDR's other leg goes to a 5V pin. Most L293D shields have 5V and GND pins next to A0-A5 (check the labels on the board).

Which LDR, which resistor?

Your pack has LDRs of different values. Use two of the same kind, so left and right behave alike. Then pick the fixed resistor to match the LDR's resistance in the room's light. That puts the reading in the middle, where it moves the most.

  1. Set a multimeter to resistance and measure one LDR in normal room light. Then cover it with your thumb and measure again, then shine a phone torch on it.
  2. Pick the fixed resistor closest to the room-light number: 4.7k, 10k, 22k or 47k.

Where they go on the robot

Put one LDR at each front corner, pointing out at 45 degrees: the left one on A2, the right one on A3.

Give each one a tube: a short piece of straw, or black tape rolled around it. Then each LDR only sees light from its own side, and the robot steers much better. Without tubes, both see the whole room and read almost the same.

Set it up (no reflashing)

  1. Open the Mecanum Workshop app and connect. On the Sensors tab, under Light seeker - wiring, tap the preset 2 front corners on A2 A3. Leave "In brighter light the reading goes" on UP.
  2. Tap Save to robot (or type kbn=2; then kw; in the console).
  3. Check: tap Watch (bw). Cover the left LDR and its number should drop. Shine a torch on it and the number should jump. Then do the right one. Stop with x.
  4. Calibrate (bc). You have 5 seconds: cover each LDR with a thumb, then shine the torch on each. This is saved on the robot.
  5. Follow the light: tap Seek on the Sensors tab or Seek light on the Drive tab (bf), or press # on the remote. Walk in front of it with a phone torch. Stop with STOP, x, or any remote key.
Run from the light (a scaredy-bot): set "In brighter light the reading goes" to DOWN (kbh=0;), even though the wiring is unchanged. The robot now thinks dark is bright. So Seek light drives it toward the darkest side, turning its nose and running away from the torch. Set it back to UP (kbh=1;) to follow the light again. You do not need to calibrate again.
If it does not work
  • "light: none in the wired layout": the number of LDRs is still 0. Pick the preset again, or type kbn=2;.
  • A number stuck near 0 or 1023: a loose wire, a missing resistor, or the sonar still plugged into A2/A3.
  • It turns away from the light when it should follow: the left and right LDRs are swapped (A2 is the left one), or the setting is on DOWN.
  • It does not move: the light is too weak. Get the torch closer, or lower the "seen" threshold (kbt=200;).
  • It stops right at the torch: that is "arrived", which is on purpose (kba, 950).

For the firmware details, see the light-seeker notes in mecanum/README.md and the CONFIG section of mecanum_holonomic.ino. You can also use 4 LDRs (A2-A5) so the robot slides toward the light from any side. That needs the gyro moved off A4/A5.
