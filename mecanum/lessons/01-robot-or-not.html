Lesson 01 · 60 minutes

Robot or Not?

What makes a machine a robot

Today's goal

Decide whether something is a robot using a test you can defend, then put our mecanum robot through that same test.

Ideas in this lesson

  • Sense → Think → Do: the loop every robot runs
  • A remote-control car does, but YOU are doing the thinking
  • The same robot can be a robot one minute and a toy the next
  • Autonomy is a slider, not a switch

Sense → Think → Do

Driven from the IR remote, the robot is all DO: you sense with your eyes and think with your head. Following a line, it senses the tape, decides how far off it is, and steers. Same wheels, same battery, different answer.

How the session runs

  1. Open Robot or Not on the big screen. Argue about five things: toaster, thermostat, Roomba, remote-control car, self-driving car. Write down the reason, not just the verdict.
  2. Look at the Sense / Think / Do columns and the exclusions (piloted, unbuilt, grown). Agree a class test: it senses something, it decides something, it acts on the world, and nobody is steering it.
  3. Meet the robot. Power it on and connect the Mecanum Workshop app: the status line says Connected, with the firmware version (fw 0.10.20), and the start-up messages scroll through the console. That is the robot saying 'my parts are here'.
  4. Drive it with the IR remote: hold an arrow to move, let go and it stops. Ask: who is sensing? Who is thinking? Who is doing? (Piloted: not a robot right now.)
  5. Press 9 on the remote for circle strafe: it circles a point ahead with its nose locked on it. Ask the questions again. Nobody is steering, but does it know where it is in the room? (No: with an IMU it only checks which way it is facing. Mostly a very good wind-up toy.)
  6. Put it on a line of tape and run line following (Sensors tab, Follow, or * on the remote). Ask the three questions one last time. Something changed.
  7. Vote: is our robot a robot? The honest answer is 'it depends what it is doing'. Mark it on the autonomy slider for each of the three runs.

Try this

  • Find a machine at home that senses but does not think. Bring it to the argument next week.
  • Is a thermostat a robot? Defend either answer: both can be right if the reason is good.
  • Add our robot to the Robot or Not table three times: remote, circle strafe, line following. Where does each land?

When it goes wrong

What you seeWhat it means
The robot ignores the remoteCheck the receiver's LED flickers when you press a key. No flicker: the remote battery or the receiver's power. Flicker but nothing happens: the signal wire is not on D2.
The app shows an orange re-flash noticeThe robot runs an older sketch than the app needs. Flash mecanum_holonomic/mecanum_holonomic.ino; the status line then shows fw 0.10.15.
Line following stops at onceExpected on day one: the sensors are not set up or calibrated yet. Use it as a question: why did the robot give up?
Notes for the grown-up

Resist settling the argument. The disagreement is the lesson: you want them to leave with a test they built, because they will use it against you later when the robot does something clever.

Ages: K-5: stick to toaster / remote car / robot vacuum and act out Sense, Think, Do with three kids holding cards. 6-12: use the full Robot or Not table, including the hair-splitting notes, and have them argue the borderline rows.

Materials: Robot (charged), IR remote, a 1 m strip of black electrical tape on a light floor, projector or big screen for the Robot or Not page, paper for verdicts.
