Lesson 08 · 60 minutes

When in Doubt, Stop

Designing a robot that fails safely

Today's goal

List every way our robot stops itself, test each one, and invent one more safety rule.

Ideas in this lesson

  • A robot must assume something will go wrong
  • Dead-man controls: let go and it stops
  • Sensors can be a safety net (the wall stop)
  • Stopping is the default answer when the robot is not sure

Sense → Think → Do

Safety is a SENSE / THINK / DO loop of its own: sense danger (a wall, a lost signal, a lost line), decide it is not sure, do the safest thing, which is usually stop.

How the session runs

  1. Hold the up arrow on the IR remote, then let go. The robot stops about a quarter second after the last signal. Why not keep going until told to stop?
  2. On the two-stick controller page, lift your thumb: that stick springs back to the middle and its part of the motion stops.
  3. Drive forward toward a wall (w, or the up arrow). The laser stops it about 120 mm away and the console says 'tof: wall at N mm'.
  4. Try driving forward again while close: it refuses. Drive backward or sideways: allowed. Why only forward?
  5. Start line following and lift the robot off the line. It stops after the lost-stop time.
  6. Start a demo, then press any remote key. Every key aborts a demo.
  7. In the app, start a demo: the red STOP bar sits at the bottom of every tab, and it keeps sending stop until the robot confirms.
  8. Group task: invent one more safety rule. What should the robot do if its battery is low? If someone picks it up?

Try this

  • What is the slowest you would let a robot near a little kid? Why?
  • The wall stop only looks forward. Where else could the robot crash?
  • Is 'stop' always the safest thing? Think of a self-driving car on a motorway.

When it goes wrong

What you seeWhat it means
Wall stop never triggersNo laser fitted, or it was not found at start-up (Sensors tab says 'not found').
Robot keeps going after letting go of the remoteThe receiver still sees repeats (another remote nearby?), or it was a key that runs a demo: press any key to abort.
STOP bar does not go awayIt waits for the robot to confirm 'stop'. Check the connection.
Notes for the grown-up

This lesson connects to the Robot or Not ethics rows. Kids are surprisingly good at inventing safety rules once they see three real ones. Keep the wall test slow (speed 3 or 4).

Ages: K-5: the let-go test and the wall test, with a soft box as the wall. 6-12: discuss failure modes (sensor dead, signal lost, software stuck) and which stop catches which.

Materials: Robot with IR receiver and laser, IR remote, a box or soft wall, tape line, app.
